# Field card: film an object so it can be measured

Most videos of objects in the sky are filmed by surprise. Many of them cannot show how far away the object was, so
its real size and speed stay unknown. A few habits while you film, and a few notes soon after, change that. They
are the same steps as on our pocket field card.

## While you film

1. **Brace the phone. Pan only if you must.** Lean the phone, or your arms, against something solid. Every shake
   moves the whole picture, and mcDonald has to measure it and take it out before it can read the object's own
   motion.
2. **Keep a cloud, tree, roof, or the horizon in view.** When you follow an object, it holds still on the screen
   while everything behind it moves. Something fixed in the picture shows how the camera turned, and so how the
   object really moved. A cloud can also show how the air was moving.
3. **Set the zoom first, then leave it alone.** A change of zoom changes how much of the sky each pixel covers.
   With one zoom, every frame is measured on the same scale.
4. **Keep filming as long as you can see it.** A longer video gives more frames to measure. It can also catch a
   change in speed, direction, or brightness.
5. **Keep the original file, uncut and unedited.** Send the file as your phone saved it. A screen recording, a
   trimmed or cropped copy, or a video passed through social media or a messaging app has lost detail and timing,
   and a frame-by-frame measurement needs both. The original file also holds the time and, often, the place.

## Then write it down

Write these down as soon as you can, while you still remember them.

**You**

- **What is the time and location?** Give the date, the time with its time zone, and where you stood, as an
  address or as latitude and longitude.
- **Were you moving?** If you were in a car, boat, or plane, note your speed and direction.

**Your view**

- **What is the compass direction?** Note the direction you faced while you filmed, from a compass or your phone's
  compass app.
- **How high was it?** Count fists up from the horizon. A fist held out at arm's length covers about 10 degrees.

**The object**

- **What is the distance to the object?** Explain how you measured or estimated it. For example, it may have passed
  in front of a hill, a building, or a cloud whose distance you know.

## Why these steps matter

A single video shows how an object moves across the picture, but not how far away it is. A small object nearby and
a large one far away can draw the same path across the screen. A fixed background and the notes above are what turn
motion on the screen into a real size and speed. Without them, the most a video can give is a range of
possibilities.

## What to do next

If you film something this way, you can send the video to us on the [Submit](submit.md) page, which asks for the
same notes. You can also measure it yourself with mcDonald, a free, deterministic, frame-by-frame motion analyzer:
see [Install](install.md).
