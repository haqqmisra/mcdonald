# Exercise 3: A known field of view (PR113)

You will measure a spot that crosses a video in about a tenth of a second, then tell mcdonald how wide
the camera sees. It then gives how fast the spot turns across the view, and what its speed would
be at each distance.

- **Time:** about 10 minutes.
- **Space:** a 67 MB download, and about 43 MB for the frames you open.
- **You need:** mcdonald 0.3.5 or newer, installed and set up ([Install](../../README.md#install)).

## The video

PR113 is three minutes of video, which the record says was likely filmed by a US military infrared
camera in 1996. In it, "an area of contrast transits the sensor field-of-view,
entering near the upper right corner and exiting near the lower left corner of the frame", at
about 0:14. You can read the whole record on
[its DVIDS page](https://www.dvidshub.net/video/1014119). You will look at two seconds of it, from
0:12.63 to 0:14.63.

The camera draws lines with numbers on them across the picture: its angle marks. From them, an
earlier analysis of this video worked out that one degree spans about 35.4 pixels on the screen,
so the picture's 1,920 pixels span about 54 degrees. That is the camera's field of view: how wide
it sees, from left to right. You will give it to mcdonald.

## In the window

1. Start the window: type `mcdonald-gui` in a terminal, or use the mcdonald icon if
   `mcdonald setup` added one.
2. On the first page, press **Open by catalog name…**, type `PR113`, and press **OK**.
3. mcdonald asks whether to download PR113 from DVIDS. Press **Yes**.
4. The page **Select the video segment** opens. Click in the box beside **Set start**, type
   `380` and press Enter. Then click in the box beside **Set end**, type `440` and press Enter.
   The line under the boxes should say 0:12.63 to 0:14.63. The spot is there for only four frames,
   408 to 411, so it is easy to miss when the video plays. Leave
   **How many objects are you looking for?** at **I don't know**, and press
   **Open this segment**.
5. mcdonald saves those frames as pictures, with a bar on the right that shows how far it has
   got.
6. In the **Measure the object** box on the right, press **Begin**. Three lights under the
   button show the steps: **Find**, **Follow** and **Measure**. Nothing is asked on the way.
   This takes about 3 minutes.
7. The report appears on the right, under the button. Its first line gives a speed in pixels a
   second: a speed on the screen. Under it is a card called **Measure the sound**, which is
   another exercise, so you can leave it for now.
8. Now give the field of view. Under the report's first lines, press **Add what you know**. In
   the box **How wide the camera sees, left to right**, type `54`. Press **Work out the speed**.
   A second or two later, three new rows are on the card.

## On the command line

These are the same steps, for a terminal or an AI agent. First, find what moves in those frames:

```bash
mcdonald look PR113 --n0 380 --n1 440 --propose
```

This downloads PR113 the first time, saves the frames, and looks for things that move against the
background. It takes about 2 minutes. It lists 7 things, best first, and draws them in the picture
`pr113/pr113_look_proposals_380_440.png`. All seven are "weak": Find is not sure of any of them,
so look at the picture yourself. Thing 1 is the dark spot seen on frames 408 to 411, moving 141
pixels each frame: the one the record describes.

Under the list, it prints a line that starts `to take one:`. Here it is, shortened:

```text
to take one:  mcdonald mark PR113 --no-window --link --set object@408=1007.9,317.3 --set object@411=700.9,605.6 --why "proposal 1 of 7 from look --propose (…); I looked at its strip and it is the object because ..."
```

Copy the command (everything after `to take one:`), replace the `...` at the end with your own
reason, and run it. It should say "4 of 4 frames linked, 408–411". Marks set this way are
recorded as an agent's choice, with your reason, and the report says so. That is expected.

Then measure it, over the same frames. This takes about 2 minutes:

```bash
mcdonald run PR113 --marks pr113/pr113_marks.json --n0 380 --n1 440 --skip integrity
```

And give it the field of view, as the window's **Work out the speed** does. This takes a few
seconds:

```bash
mcdonald report pr113/pr113_case.json --fov 54
```

To have an AI agent do it, you can give it this prompt:

> Please use the mcdonald toolkit to analyze the PR113 video released under PURSUE, following
> https://github.com/haqqmisra/mcdonald/blob/main/docs/exercises/03-pr113.md. Run
> `mcdonald readme` to get started.

## What you should get

Both ways give the same conclusion and the same numbers. The report starts, before and after you
give the field of view:

> **No physical conclusion.** The object moved about 4269 pixels a second in the image, and its real speed and size cannot be found from this video alone.

The window's report card also says it followed the spot on 4 frames (frames 408 to 411), at
142.3 pixels a frame. After you give the field of view, three rows are added: **Field of view**
54.0°, **Scale** 1884 px/rad, and **Turn rate** 2.27 rad/s. The question on the card changes to
"Its real speed needs how far away the object is".

## What it means

- **From pixels to angles.** Once mcdonald knows that the picture spans 54 degrees, it can turn
  pixels into angles. The spot turns across the view at 2.27 radians a second, about 130 degrees
  a second: it crosses the whole picture in less than half a second.
- **A speed still needs a distance.** How fast something turns across your view depends on how
  far away it is: a fly near your face and a plane far off can turn across it at the same rate.
  The speed is the turn rate times the distance. The record gives no distance, so the first line
  of the report does not change.
- **What it would be at each distance.** Press **Full report** and find the picture called
  "Size (left) and speed (right) against distance". Its one line says how big the spot would be,
  and how fast it would go, at each distance. A 2-meter weather balloon would have to be about
  180 meters away, going about 400 meters a second: faster than sound. A 17-meter fighter jet
  would be about 1.5 kilometers away, going about 3,500 meters a second. Unless the spot was small
  and close, it was very fast.
- **54 degrees and the angle marks.** The earlier analysis gave a turn rate of 2.10 radians a
  second, from the angle marks themselves. mcdonald turns a field of view into pixels the way a
  camera lens does, and that differs a little from stretching the marks straight across the
  picture, so it gives 2.27. On the command line you can give the marks instead of the field of
  view: `mcdonald report pr113/pr113_case.json --graticule 35.4` gives 2.10.
- **Check the track by eye.** The report card says **Track not yet checked by eye**. Press
  **Full report** and find the track sheet: a small picture of each frame, with a ring where
  mcdonald followed the spot. If the ring is on the spot in all four frames, press **I looked**.
  On the command line, open `pr113/pr113_all_frames.jpg`, then run
  `mcdonald report pr113/pr113_case.json --i-looked`.
