# Exercise 1: Birdwatching (PR169)

You will open a short video by its name, let mcdonald do the whole job with one button, and see
how it tells a bird by the beat of its wings.

- **Time:** about 5 to 10 minutes, nearly all of it the computer working.
- **Space:** a 3 MB download, and about 0.1 GB for the frames.
- **You need:** mcdonald 0.5.10 or newer, installed and set up ([Install](../../README.md#install)).

## The video

PR169 is 14 seconds of infrared video from a US military camera, filmed in the Middle East in
2023 and released in November 2026. The record says only that the camera "pans to track an area
of contrast, keeping it generally within the center of the frame". You can read the whole record
on [its DVIDS page](https://www.dvidshub.net/video/1030277). You will look at all of it.

## In the window

1. Start the window: type `mcdonald-gui` in a terminal, or use the mcdonald icon if
   `mcdonald setup` added one.
2. On the first page, press **Open by catalog name…**, type `PR169`, and press **OK**.
3. mcdonald asks whether to download PR169 from DVIDS. Press **Yes**.
4. The page **Select the video segment** opens: a segment is the stretch of video you will
   measure. This video is short, so keep all of it. The line under the boxes should say
   0:00.00 to 0:13.58. Press **Play segment** to watch it: a small bright spot crosses the
   picture and turns twice, while the camera follows it. Leave
   **How many objects are you looking for?** at **I don't know**, and press
   **Open this segment**.
5. mcdonald saves the frames as pictures, with a bar on the right that shows how far it has
   got.
6. In the **Measure the object** box on the right, press **Begin**. Three lights under the
   button show the steps. **Find** looks for spots that move against the background,
   **Follow** follows the likeliest one from frame to frame, and **Measure** works out how it
   moved and writes a report. Nothing is asked on the way. This is the long part: about 4 to
   8 minutes.
7. The report appears on the right, under the button, with a small bird beside its first line.
   Under it is a card called **Measure the sound**. It measures the sound track, which is another
   exercise, so you can leave it for now.

## On the command line

These are the same three steps, for a terminal or an AI agent. First, find what moves:

```bash
mcdonald look PR169 --propose
```

This downloads PR169 the first time, saves the frames, and looks for spots that move against the
background. It takes a minute or two. It lists 3 spots that move, best first, and draws them in
the picture `pr169/pr169_look_proposals_1_408.png`. Open that picture. Spot 1 is the bright one
the camera follows. The other two are weak: each was seen on only 3 frames.

Under the list, it prints a line that starts `to take one:`. Here it is, shortened:

```text
to take one:  mcdonald mark PR169 --no-window --link --set object@3=526.0,657.0 … --set object@406=900.0,532.0 --why "proposal 1 of 3 from look --propose (…); I looked at its strip and it is the object because ..."
```

Copy the command (everything after `to take one:`), replace the `...` after "because" with your own
reason, and run it. It puts marks on spot 1, the points that say where the object is in a frame,
and follows it from frame to frame. The marks go in the video's folder, `pr169/`, beside the
picture. It should say "396 of 408 frames linked, 1–408". On the other 12 frames it could not
see the spot. Marks set this way are recorded as an agent's choice, with your reason, and the
report says so. That is expected.

Then measure it:

```bash
mcdonald run PR169 --marks pr169/pr169_marks.json --skip integrity
```

`--skip integrity` leaves out the slowest check, as the window does. This takes about
3 minutes. The report is `pr169/pr169_case.md`.

To have an AI agent do it, you can give it this prompt:

> Please use the mcdonald toolkit to analyze the PR169 video released under PURSUE, following
> https://github.com/haqqmisra/mcdonald/blob/main/docs/exercises/01-pr169.md. Run
> `mcdonald readme` to get started.

## What you should get

Both ways give the same conclusion and the same numbers. The report starts:

> **Beats like a bird.** A bird is the leading explanation. Its brightness rises and falls 5.9 times a second, the rate of a wingbeat. It also beats 11.9 times a second, its double. That rhythm is its own, not the video's. A tumbling body or a blinking light would beat too, and nothing here tells them apart. The object moved about 72 pixels a second in the picture, as the camera held it. The picture behind it held still on screen, so its motion against the background is not measured. Its real speed and size cannot be found from this video alone.

The window's report card also says it followed the spot on 396 frames (frames 1 to 408), and
that the beat is 5.9 Hz. There should be one report card. If you see two or three, each for a
part of the video, your mcdonald is older than 0.5.10.

## What it means

- **A beat is a regular rise and fall in brightness.** As a bird's wings go up and down, it shows
  the camera more and less of itself, so it looks brighter and dimmer in turn. Here the spot does
  that about 5.9 times a second (5.9 Hz). mcdonald takes 2 to 25 times a second as a wingbeat's
  rate: large birds beat their wings slowly, small ones fast.
- **And its double.** A bird's brightness can change twice in each stroke of its wings, once on
  the way up and once on the way down. The rhythm then shows at the wingbeat's rate and at twice
  that rate, here 5.9 and 11.9 Hz. The report names the slower one as the wingbeat.
- **Its own, not the video's.** Press **Full report** and find the picture with a blue line and
  a grey one. The blue line is the spot's brightness, which rises and falls in a steady rhythm.
  The grey line is the background just beside it, which stays nearly flat. On the right, the blue
  line has two tall, narrow peaks, at 5.93 and 11.86 Hz. The dotted line marks the video's own
  rhythm, which comes from the way the file was saved. Neither peak is on it, so the beat belongs
  to the spot.
- **The leading explanation, not proof.** A tumbling body or a blinking light also beats.
  The report says so, and calls a bird the leading explanation, not the answer.
- **One object, found three times.** The bird turns twice. Each time it slows down on the screen,
  and Find loses it for a moment. So Find sees it three separate times: frames 3 to 39, 68 to 251
  and 261 to 406. Find then checks whether the spot can be followed from each of these into the
  next. It can, so the list shows one spot and the report is about one object.
- **The speed is only on the screen.** The camera follows the spot, so it moves only about
  72 pixels a second in the picture. A real speed needs three more quantities: how wide the camera
  sees, how far away the spot was, and how the camera moved. The record gives none of these, so
  mcdonald does not guess.
- **Check the track by eye.** The track is the spot's position in every frame, joined up. The
  report card says **Track not yet checked by eye**. Press **Full report** and find the track
  sheet: a small picture of every frame, with a ring where mcdonald followed the spot. If the
  ring is on the spot in every frame, press **I looked**. On the command line, the sheet is six
  pictures, `pr169/pr169_all_frames_p1.jpg` to `pr169/pr169_all_frames_p6.jpg`. Open them, then
  run `mcdonald report pr169/pr169_case.json --i-looked`. Nothing is measured again. The report
  stops calling its numbers not yet sure.
