# Exercise 4: Six at once (PR135)

You will tell mcdonald how many objects you are looking for, and see what it does with the number:
here it takes a flock apart, and gives each bird a report of its own.

- **Time:** about 35 to 45 minutes, nearly all of it the computer working.
- **Space:** a 23 MB download, and about 50 MB for the frames you open.
- **You need:** mcdonald 0.3.10 or newer, installed and set up ([Install](../../README.md#install)).

## The video

PR135 is one minute of infrared video from a US military camera, filmed in 2025. The report that
came with it describes "six small spherical objects grouped together" that "seem[ingly]" were
"agile" and "frequently changing direction", and the person who reported them put their speed at
480 miles an hour. The record says the camera turns to follow them from 0:11 to 0:47. You can read
the whole record on [its DVIDS page](https://www.dvidshub.net/video/1023397). You will look at five
seconds of it, from 0:41.34 to 0:46.31.

The report says six, so this time you tell mcdonald that you are looking for six objects.

## In the window

1. Start the window: type `mcdonald-gui` in a terminal, or use the mcdonald icon if
   `mcdonald setup` added one.
2. On the first page, press **Open by catalog name…**, type `PR135`, and press **OK**.
3. mcdonald asks whether to download PR135 from DVIDS. Press **Yes**.
4. The page **Select the video segment** opens. Click in the box beside **Set start**, type
   `1240` and press Enter. Then click in the box beside **Set end**, type `1389` and press Enter.
   The line under the boxes should say 0:41.34 to 0:46.31. Press **Play segment** to watch it: two
   small clusters of bright spots, below and to the left of the middle, drift to the left
   together.
5. Now the number. Click in the box beside **How many objects are you looking for?**, where it
   says **I don't know**, type `6` and press Enter. Then press **Open this segment**.
6. mcdonald saves those frames as pictures, with a bar on the right that shows how far it has
   got.
7. In the **Measure the object** box on the right, press **Begin**. Three lights under the
   button show the steps: **Find**, **Follow** and **Measure**. Nothing is asked on the way. This
   takes about 30 to 40 minutes: mcdonald measures each thing it found, and then each bird on its
   own.
8. When it ends, the line under the button holds your number against what was followed, and the
   right column has a report card for each object. Scroll down to see them all.

## On the command line

These are the same steps, for a terminal or an AI agent. First, find what moves in those frames,
and say how many objects you are looking for:

```bash
mcdonald look PR135 --n0 1240 --n1 1389 --propose --objects 6
```

This downloads PR135 the first time, saves the frames, and looks for things that move against the
background. It takes about 3 minutes. It lists 3 things, best first, and draws them in the picture
`pr135/segment-1240-1389/pr135_look_proposals_1240_1389.png`, in the segment's own folder. Thing 1
is "strong", thing 2 "fair" and thing 3 "weak". Things 1 and 2 are the two clusters, seen on 87 and 82 frames; thing 3 was seen on only
3. Each line says the thing "holds about 3 points" or "about 2 points, not one": it may be a group.
Then it says that you are looking for 6 objects and 3 things were found, and that a group of points
can be split into its members.

Under the list, it prints `to take the 3 likeliest:` and three commands, one for each thing. Each
ends with `--segment 1240-1389`, which would put its marks in the segment's folder. Copy each one,
replace the `...` after "because" with your own reason, and replace `--segment 1240-1389` with
`--out pr135/segment-1240-1389/object-1` in the first, `object-2` in the second and `object-3` in
the third, so that each thing has a folder of its own inside the segment's. Here they are,
shortened, with that done:

```text
mcdonald mark PR135 --no-window --link --set object@1281=420.0,635.0 … --set object@1387=257.0,640.0 --why "proposal 1 of 3 from look --propose (…); I looked at its strip and it is the object because ..." --out pr135/segment-1240-1389/object-1
mcdonald mark PR135 --no-window --link --set object@1256=381.0,578.0 … --set object@1351=121.0,596.0 --why "proposal 2 of 3 from look --propose (…); I looked at its strip and it is the object because ..." --out pr135/segment-1240-1389/object-2
mcdonald mark PR135 --no-window --link --set object@1242=428.0,518.0 --set object@1246=433.0,549.0 --why "proposal 3 of 3 from look --propose (…); I looked at its strip and it is the object because ..." --out pr135/segment-1240-1389/object-3
```

Run each, in turn. They should say
"151 of 151 frames linked, 1251–1401", "156 of 156 frames linked, 1226–1381" and
"65 of 65 frames linked, 1212–1276". Marks set this way are recorded as an agent's choice, with
your reason, and the reports say so. That is expected.

Then measure them all, over the same frames, with the same number. This takes about 30 minutes:

```bash
mcdonald run PR135 --each pr135/segment-1240-1389 --objects 6 --n0 1240 --n1 1389 --skip integrity
```

`--skip integrity` leaves out the slowest check, as the window does. The list of the objects and
what each report says is `pr135/segment-1240-1389/pr135_objects.md`.

To have an AI agent do it, you can give it this prompt:

> Please use the mcdonald toolkit to analyze the PR135 video released under PURSUE, following
> https://github.com/haqqmisra/mcdonald/blob/main/docs/exercises/04-pr135.md. Run
> `mcdonald readme` to get started.

## What it means

- **The number changes what mcdonald does.** Find saw three things: two that each hold a few
  points, and a third seen on only 3 frames. Left at **I don't know**, the one button follows the
  two groups and measures each as one flock, in about 11 minutes, and you get two report cards.
  Told 6, it takes every thing Find listed, weak ones too. It found fewer things than 6, and two of
  them are groups, so each point of a group that was seen long enough for a beat becomes an object
  of its own. That is how you get a report for each bird.
- **Six birds.** Each of objects 4 to 9 beats its brightness at its own rate, between 7.1 and 7.9
  times a second, out of step with the others. That is the rate of a small bird's wingbeat. An
  earlier analysis of this video found the same: six birds, each beating its wings about 7 to 8
  times a second. The flock's reports say why it cannot be the video's own rhythm: that would beat
  every bird at once.
- **Counted once.** Object 3 is the third thing Find saw, on 3 frames. Followed from there, it is
  one of the second group's birds, seen before that bird was followed on its own as object 8: on
  every frame both were followed on, the two are on the same pixels. So it is the same bird,
  followed twice, and mcdonald counts it once. Its report is still in its folder, and its part of
  the list says why it is not counted.
- **The number is a hint, not an order.** If mcdonald finds fewer things than you said, it tells
  you how many were not found. If it finds more, it tells you that too. It never makes up a thing
  to reach your number.
- **Not lanterns, as far as this goes.** Press **Full report** on object 2. A sky lantern's flame
  flickers at rates like these too, but lanterns let go together drift on one wind and keep their
  places. This group does not keep its shape: it moves around the way a flock does.
- **The speed is only on the screen.** The camera follows the birds, and nothing in the video
  says how far away they were. The report says what it would need.
- **Check the tracks by eye.** Each report card says **Track not yet checked by eye**. Press
  **Full report** on each, find its track sheet, and if the ring is on the bird in every frame,
  press **I looked**. On the command line, open each
  `pr135/segment-1240-1389/object-N/pr135_all_frames.jpg`, then run
  `mcdonald report pr135/segment-1240-1389/object-4/pr135_case.json --i-looked` for object 4, and
  the same for the others.
