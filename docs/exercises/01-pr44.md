# Exercise 1: Birdwatching (PR44)

You will open part of a video by its name, let mcdonald do the whole job with one button, and see
how it tells a bird by the beat of its wings.

- **Time:** about 25 to 35 minutes, nearly all of it the computer working.
- **Space:** an 87 MB download, and about 0.3 GB for the frames you open.
- **You need:** mcdonald 0.3.4 or newer, installed and set up ([Install](../../README.md#install)).

## The video

PR44 is five minutes of infrared video from a US military camera, filmed in 2020. The person who
reported it gave no description. The record says the camera follows "an area of contrast" for
about three minutes, and then zooms out while it keeps following it. You can read the whole record
on [its DVIDS page](https://www.dvidshub.net/video/1006104). You will look at 14 seconds of it,
from 4:25.97 to 4:39.97, as the camera zooms out.

The video also has sound. The record says the sound "does not relate to the visual content", so
this exercise leaves it out.

## In the window

1. Start the window: type `mcdonald-gui` in a terminal, or use the mcdonald icon if
   `mcdonald setup` added one.
2. On the first page, press **Open by catalog name…**, type `PR44`, and press **OK**.
3. mcdonald asks whether to download PR44 from DVIDS. Press **Yes**.
4. The page **Select the video segment** opens. Click in the box beside **Set start**, type
   `7980` and press Enter. Then click in the box beside **Set end**, type `8400` and press Enter.
   The line under the boxes should say 4:25.97 to 4:39.97. Press **Play segment** to watch it: a
   small bright spot drifts across the middle of the picture while the view widens. Leave
   **How many objects are you looking for?** at **I don't know**, and press
   **Open this segment**.
5. mcdonald saves those frames as pictures, with a bar on the right that shows how far it has
   got.
6. Press **Find, follow and measure the object**. Three lights under the button show the steps.
   **Find** looks for things that move against the background, **Follow** follows the likeliest
   one from frame to frame, and **Measure** works out how it moved and writes a report. Nothing
   is asked on the way. This is the long part: about 20 to 25 minutes.
7. The report appears on the right, under the button, with a small bird beside its first line.
   Under it is a card called **Measure the sound**. It measures the sound track, which is another
   exercise, so you can leave it for now.

## On the command line

These are the same three steps, for a terminal or an AI agent. First, find what moves in those
frames:

```bash
mcdonald look PR44 --n0 7980 --n1 8400 --propose
```

This downloads PR44 the first time, saves the frames, and looks for things that move against the
background. It takes about 7 minutes. It lists 3 things, best first, and draws them in the
picture `pr44/pr44_look_proposals_7980_8400.png`. Open that picture. Thing 1 is the bright spot
the camera follows.

Under the list, it prints a line that starts `to take one:`. Here it is, shortened:

```text
to take one:  mcdonald mark PR44 --no-window --link --set object@7983=690.0,542.0 … --set object@8397=1043.0,582.0 --why "proposal 1 of 3 from look --propose (…); I looked at its strip and it is the object because ..."
```

Copy the command (everything after `to take one:`), replace the `...` at the end with your own
reason, and run it. It puts marks on thing 1 and follows it from frame to frame. It should say
"446 of 446 frames linked, 7982–8427": it follows the spot a little past the end of your
segment, and the next step measures only the frames you chose. Marks set this way are recorded
as an agent's choice, with your reason, and the report says so. That is expected.

Then measure it, over the same frames:

```bash
mcdonald run PR44 --marks pr44/pr44_marks.json --n0 7980 --n1 8400 --skip integrity
```

`--skip integrity` leaves out the slowest check, as the window does. This takes about
20 minutes. The report is `pr44/pr44_case.md`.

To have an AI agent do it, you can give it this prompt:

> Please use the mcdonald toolkit to analyze the PR44 video released under PURSUE, following
> https://github.com/haqqmisra/mcdonald/blob/main/docs/exercises/01-pr44.md. Run
> `mcdonald readme` to get started.

## What you should get

Both ways give the same conclusion and the same numbers. The report starts:

> **Beats like a bird.** A bird is the leading explanation: its brightness beats at 3.1 Hz, the rate of a wingbeat, a rhythm of its own and not the video's (a tumbling body or a blinking light would beat too, and nothing here tells them apart); the object moved about 22 pixels a second against the background, and its real speed and size cannot be found from this video alone; something moves with it.

The window's report card also says it followed the spot on 419 frames (frames 7982 to 8400),
and that the beat is 3.1 Hz. If your segment starts or ends a little differently, for example
because you dragged the handles, the speed may come out a pixel or two different, but the beat
should still be 3.1 Hz. If it starts a second or more earlier, Find may also take a brief bright
flash near the start as a second object, and you get two report cards: the bird's says the same
as above, and the flash's says **No physical conclusion**.

## What it means

- **A beat is a rise and fall in brightness.** As a bird's wings go up and down, it shows the
  camera more and less of itself, so it looks brighter and dimmer in turn. Here the spot does
  that about 3.1 times a second. mcdonald takes 2 to 25 times a second as a wingbeat's rate:
  large birds beat their wings slowly, small ones fast.
- **Its own, not the video's.** Press **Full report** and find the picture with a blue line and
  a grey one. The blue line is the spot's brightness, which rises and falls in a steady rhythm by
  about a quarter. The grey line is the background just beside it, which stays flat. On the
  right, the blue line has one tall, narrow peak at 3.07 Hz. The dotted line is the video's own
  rhythm, from the way it was saved, and the peak is far from it. So the beat belongs to the spot.
- **The leading explanation, not proof.** A tumbling body or a blinking light also beats.
  The report says so, and calls a bird the leading explanation, not the answer.
- **Something moves with it.** mcdonald found a dark spot about 14 pixels from the bright one
  that stays with it through the stretch. It notes it, and does not say what it is.
- **The speed is only on the screen.** The camera follows the spot, so it moves only about
  22 pixels a second in the picture. To turn that into a real speed you need how wide the camera
  sees, how far away the spot was, and how the camera moved. The record gives none of these, so
  mcdonald does not guess.
- **Check the track by eye.** The report card says **Track not yet checked by eye**. Press
  **Full report** and find the track sheet: a small picture of every frame, with a ring where
  mcdonald followed the spot. If the ring is on the spot in every frame, press **I looked**. On
  the command line, open `pr44/pr44_all_frames.jpg`, then run
  `mcdonald report pr44/pr44_case.json --i-looked`. Nothing is measured again. The report
  stops calling its numbers not yet sure.
