# Exercise 2: A ship for a ruler (PR149)

You will measure a fast spot that crosses a video, then use a ship in the same picture as a ruler,
so that mcdonald can turn its speed on the screen into a real speed.

- **Time:** about 15 to 20 minutes, nearly all of it the computer working.
- **Space:** a 55 MB download, and about 0.1 GB for the frames you open.
- **You need:** mcdonald 0.3.5 or newer, installed and set up ([Install](../../README.md#install)).

## The video

PR149 is 51 seconds of infrared video from a US military camera, filmed in 2023. The record says
that in the first four seconds "an area of contrast transits the frame from the bottom right edge
to the lower left edge", and that the camera then turns to follow it. You can read the whole
record on [its DVIDS page](https://www.dvidshub.net/video/1017799). You will look at the first
five seconds, from 0:00.00 to 0:04.97.

A cargo ship sits in the middle of the picture. It looks like a bulk carrier of the size called
Handymax, which is 150 to 200 meters long. This exercise takes 175 meters, the middle of that.
On the screen the ship is about 920 pixels long, as an earlier analysis of this video measured it.

## In the window

1. Start the window: type `mcdonald-gui` in a terminal, or use the mcdonald icon if
   `mcdonald setup` added one.
2. On the first page, press **Open by catalog name…**, type `PR149`, and press **OK**.
3. mcdonald asks whether to download PR149 from DVIDS. Press **Yes**.
4. The page **Select the video segment** opens. The box beside **Set start** already says frame 1.
   Click in the box beside **Set end**, type `150` and press Enter. The line under the boxes
   should say 0:00.00 to 0:04.97. Press **Play segment** to watch it: a small bright spot crosses
   from right to left, low in the picture, below the ship. Leave
   **How many objects are you looking for?** at **I don't know**, and press
   **Open this segment**.
5. mcdonald saves those frames as pictures, with a bar on the right that shows how far it has
   got.
6. In the **Measure the object** box on the right, press **Begin**. Three lights under the
   button show the steps: **Find**, **Follow** and **Measure**. Nothing is asked on the way.
   This takes about 10 to 15 minutes.
7. The report appears on the right, under the button. Its first line gives a speed in pixels a
   second: a speed on the screen.
8. Now use the ship. Under the report's first lines, press **Add what you know**. Fill in two
   boxes:
   - **How long something in the picture really is**: type `175`, and change the unit beside it
     from feet to **meters**.
   - **and how long it is on the screen**: type `920`. (Or press **Measure on the video** and drag
     along the ship from one end to the other. Your own measurement goes in the box, and the
     speed then comes out a little different.)

   Press **Work out the speed**. A second or two later the card has a speed in meters a second.

## On the command line

These are the same steps, for a terminal or an AI agent. First, find what moves in those frames:

```bash
mcdonald look PR149 --n0 1 --n1 150 --propose
```

This downloads PR149 the first time, saves the frames, and looks for things that move against the
background. It takes about 3 minutes. It lists 3 things, best first, and draws them in the picture
`pr149/pr149_look_proposals_1_150.png`. Thing 1 is "strong": the bright spot that crosses the
picture. Things 2 and 3 are "weak".

Under the list, it prints a line that starts `to take one:`. Here it is, shortened:

```text
to take one:  mcdonald mark PR149 --no-window --link --set object@17=1637.5,893.2 … --set object@95=78.0,669.0 --why "proposal 1 of 3 from look --propose (…); I looked at its strip and it is the object because ..."
```

Copy the command (everything after `to take one:`), replace the `...` at the end with your own
reason, and run it. It should say "70 of 81 frames linked, 16–96". Marks set this way are recorded
as an agent's choice, with your reason, and the report says so. That is expected.

Then measure it, over the same frames. This takes about 8 minutes:

```bash
mcdonald run PR149 --marks pr149/pr149_marks.json --n0 1 --n1 150 --skip integrity
```

And give it the ship, as the window's **Work out the speed** does. This takes a few seconds:

```bash
mcdonald report pr149/pr149_case.json --ref-px 920 --ref-m 175
```

To have an AI agent do it, you can give it this prompt:

> Please use the mcdonald toolkit to analyze the PR149 video released under PURSUE, following
> https://github.com/haqqmisra/mcdonald/blob/main/docs/exercises/02-pr149.md. Run
> `mcdonald readme` to get started.

## What you should get

Both ways give the same conclusion and the same numbers. Before you add the ship, the report
starts:

> **No physical conclusion.** The object moved about 604 pixels a second in the image, and its real speed and size cannot be found from this video alone; something moves with it.

After you add the ship, it starts:

> **Subsonic motion.** The object moved at most about 115 m/s across the line of sight, relative to the camera's platform, if it is no farther away than the thing of known size: under the speed of sound (Mach 0.33); something moves with it.

The window's report card also says it followed the spot on 70 frames (frames 16 to 96), at
20.1 pixels a frame. After you add the ship, a row says **Relative speed, across: 115 m/s ×
R_obj/R_ref**: 115 m/s, times how much farther the spot is than the ship.

## What it means

- **No physical conclusion, until the ship.** Pixels a second are a speed on the screen, not a
  real one, so the first report has no physical conclusion. The ship gives the meters.
- **The ship is a ruler.** At the ship, 920 pixels of the screen are 175 meters. The spot moves
  604 pixels a second, which is about two thirds of a ship's length a second. At the ship's
  distance that is about 115 meters a second, or about 260 miles an hour.
- **Only at the ship's distance.** A ruler in the picture works only at its own distance. If the
  spot is nearer the camera than the ship, the same pixels are fewer meters, and it is slower. If
  it is farther away, it is faster. The report says so in its first line.
- **Subsonic motion.** So if the spot is no farther away than the ship, 115 m/s is the most it
  can be moving across the picture: about a third of the speed of sound (Mach 0.33). It is not
  moving faster than sound across the line of sight. This is the main thing the ship tells you,
  as the JAIS Technical Note showed for this video. Its motion toward or away from the camera is
  not measured.
- **The ship's length is a choice.** Press **Change** on the card, type `150` for the ship's
  length, and press **Work out the speed** again: about 98 m/s. With `200` it is about 131 m/s.
  Either way it is under the speed of sound. The answer is only as good as what you give it, and
  the report keeps what you gave.
- **It is a relative speed.** The aircraft with the camera was moving too, and this speed
  includes its motion.
- **Not yet checked by eye.** The card has a line that says nobody has checked the track by eye
  yet, and the report says so at its top. Press **Full report** and find the track sheet: a small
  picture of every frame, with a ring where mcdonald followed the spot. If the ring is on the spot
  in every frame, press **I looked**, and the line goes away. On the command line, open
  `pr149/pr149_all_frames.jpg`, then run `mcdonald report pr149/pr149_case.json --i-looked`.
- **Something moves with it.** mcdonald found a dark spot that stays with the bright one along the
  way. It notes it, and does not say what it is.
