# Exercise 1: Your first video (PR23)

You will open a video by its name, let mcdonald do the whole job with one button, and read what
its report can tell you and what it cannot.

- **Time:** about 20 to 25 minutes, nearly all of it the computer working.
- **Space:** a 5 MB download, and about 0.2 GB for the video's frames.
- **You need:** mcdonald 0.3.2 or newer, installed and set up ([Install](../../README.md#install)).

## The video

PR23 is ten seconds of infrared video, filmed by a US military camera looking down on a town in
Iraq in December 2022. Its record says a mission report described the object as "flying west to
east", and that the video shows "an area of contrast moving from the bottom left to the top
right" that then leaves the picture near the top right corner. You can read the whole record on
[its DVIDS page](https://www.dvidshub.net/video/1006062).

## In the window

1. Start the window: type `mcdonald-gui` in a terminal, or use the mcdonald icon if
   `mcdonald setup` added one.
2. On the first page, press **Open by catalog name…**, type `PR23`, and press **OK**.
3. mcdonald asks whether to download PR23 from DVIDS. Press **Yes**.
4. The page **Select the video segment** opens with the whole video chosen, frame 1 to
   frame 322. Press play (▶) under the video and watch it once. A small bright spot crosses
   from the bottom left toward the top right, and leaves the picture after about seven seconds.
   Leave the segment as it is, leave **How many objects are you looking for?** at
   **I don't know**, and press **Open this segment**. (If PR23 was opened on this computer
   before, press **Whole video** first.)
5. mcdonald saves the video's frames as pictures, with a bar on the right that shows how far it
   has got.
6. Press **Find, follow and measure the object**. Three lights under the button show the steps.
   **Find** looks for things that move against the background, **Follow** follows the likeliest
   one from frame to frame, and **Measure** works out how it moved and writes a report. Nothing
   is asked on the way. This is the long part: about 15 to 20 minutes.
7. The report appears on the right, under the button.

## On the command line

These are the same three steps, for a terminal or an AI agent. First, find what moves:

```bash
mcdonald look PR23 --propose
```

This downloads PR23 the first time, saves its frames, and looks for things that move against the
background. It takes about 4 minutes. It lists 3 things, best first, and draws them in the picture
`pr23/pr23_look_proposals_1_322.png`. Open that picture. Thing 1 is "strong": the bright spot that
crosses the video. Things 2 and 3 are "weak", and each is seen for less than a second.

Under the list, it prints a line that starts `to take one:`. Here it is, shortened:

```text
to take one:  mcdonald mark PR23 --no-window --link --set object@3=415.0,1000.0 … --set object@200=1850.0,233.0 --why "proposal 1 of 3 from look --propose (…); I looked at its strip and it is the object because ..."
```

Copy the command (everything after `to take one:`), replace the `...` at the end with your own
reason, and run it. It puts marks on thing 1 and follows it from frame to frame. It should say
"202 of 204 frames linked, 3–206". Marks set this way are recorded as an agent's choice, with
your reason, and the report says so. That is expected.

Then measure it:

```bash
mcdonald run PR23 --marks pr23/pr23_marks.json --skip integrity
```

`--skip integrity` leaves out the slowest check, as the window does. This takes 15 to
20 minutes. The report is `pr23/pr23_case.md`.

To have an AI agent do it, you can give it this prompt:

> Please use the mcdonald toolkit to analyze the PR23 video released under PURSUE, following
> https://github.com/haqqmisra/mcdonald/blob/main/docs/exercises/01-pr23.md. Run
> `mcdonald readme` to get started.

## What you should get

Both ways give the same conclusion and the same numbers. The report starts:

> **No physical conclusion.** The object moved about 242 pixels a second against the background, and its real speed and size cannot be found from this video alone.

The window's report card also says it followed the object on 202 frames (frames 3 to 206), at
8.2 pixels a frame in the picture.

## What it means

- **Pixels a second is a speed on the screen, not in the sky.** A plane far away and a bird
  close by can cross the screen at the same speed. To turn 242 pixels a second into a real speed,
  you need to know how wide the camera sees and how far away the object was. The record gives
  neither, so mcdonald does not guess. "No physical conclusion" is the right answer here.
- **Two kinds of background.** The report also measures the spot against two kinds of pattern in
  the background, which it calls layers: stripy ("striated") and even ("isotropic"). Over a sea
  under clouds these can move quite differently in the picture. Here they agree closely: 8.3 and
  8.1 pixels a frame.
- **No wingbeat.** The full report's row "brightness beat" finds no beat: the spot's
  brightness does not rise and fall the way a bird's does as it beats its wings. The frames where
  the spot crosses a warm roof and is lost against it (131 to 136, and 138) are left out.
- **Check the track by eye.** The report card says **Track not yet checked by eye**. Press
  **Full report** and find the track sheet: a small picture of every frame, with a ring where
  mcdonald followed the spot. If the ring is on the spot in every frame, press **I looked**. On
  the command line, open `pr23/pr23_all_frames.jpg`, then run
  `mcdonald report pr23/pr23_case.json --i-looked`. Nothing is measured again. The report
  stops calling its numbers not yet sure.

If you want to see more, turn on **Advanced** under the button and do the three steps one at a
time. Find then shows its list of the 3 things it found.
