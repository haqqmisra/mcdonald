# mcdonald 0.2.0 — issues and feature requests from an agent run (PR135, 2026-09-22)

First end-to-end use of the CLI by an AI agent (Claude Code, no display), on
DOW-UAP-PR135 (R06, MQ-9, 6 hot points, D108 "480 mph"). Jobs went through
Slurm on merlin. Numbered in the order they came up. Severity: **B** bug,
**F** feature, **D** docs/ergonomics.

## Priorities (my ranking after the whole run)

1. **Item 19, `layers` registers fixed-pattern banding (serious bug).** On banded IR
   clips every "against the background" rate is really against the screen, and camera
   motion reads as zero. It blocked the one remaining PR135 test.
2. **Item 1, a parallax/ground-projection ladder in `kinematics`.** This is the
   question behind every MQ-9 "speed" in R06 and the one the council actually asked.
3. **Items 21/24, flicker (wingbeat) photometry, with the codec, pixel-phase and
   common-window controls built in.** It was the most decisive new evidence here,
   and it has two traps that each fake a result.
4. **Items 3/8/22, groups and sensor defects.** Multi-member tracks, a
   rigid-vs-shuffling test, and a defect map.
5. **Item 9, `symbology` auto should fall back to a template, or fail fast.** It spent 8 min, then exited 5.

**What worked well, for an agent with no display.** `look` overview → `look --propose`
found the group unaided (item 6). The `to_accept` command plus `--why` made the
agent-mark record honest with no effort. `kinematics` reproduced the hand workup to
0.2 px/s and refused m/s (item 13). `symbology --method template` gave 600/600 frames
at 0.27 % radius scatter (item 16). The JSON envelope with `no_power`/`needs` was
easy to consume from Slurm jobs, and the frame cache under `TMPDIR` was shared
between jobs. Exit codes were accurate: 5 for "pointer not found", 0 elsewhere,
with no tracebacks from `mcdonald` itself.

**How it was run.** A one-line `sbatch` wrapper (`mc.sbatch`: `mcdonald "$@" --json >
json/<job>.json`) with `TMPDIR=/scratch/tmp/...`, 1 CPU per job because the node was
full. `look`/`propose` took ~3–20 min on 1 CPU for 180–1100 frames; `symbology` took 4–8 min
for the full clip.

## Items, in the order they came up

1. **F: no parallax / terrain-locked-projection model.** The PR135 question, and
   the one behind every MQ-9 "speed" in R06, is whether a ground-projected speed
   is the object's own speed or own-ship parallax: v_G = k·v_O − (k−1)·v_A,
   k = h_A/(h_A−h_O). `kinematics` has `--range-ratio`, but it has no own-ship
   velocity and no way to take a *reported* speed and ask which (k, v_O) it
   permits. Suggest `mcdonald kinematics --reported-speed V --own-ship V_A[,HDG]
   --los-az AZ`, printing a k and h_O/h_A ladder for v_O in {0, bird, …}. The
   KIAS→TAS conversion (ISA, a ±15 °C band) belongs with it. I did this by hand in
   a heredoc.
2. **D: `look` gives no timing hint.** `look --frame N` took 23 s for 61 lossless
   720p frames, and nothing told me in advance. On a busy shared node an agent
   has to choose between srun and running inline. The cost line that
   `--frame` prints should come before the extraction, not only in
   `docs/agents.md`.
3. **F: a group has no class.** The mark classes are `object` and `object2`.
   PR135 is 5–6 points moving as a group, like the "fleet" cases. There is no
   way to mark or link a formation, or to ask whether its members keep their
   relative positions (rigid vs. shuffling), which is the discriminator between
   birds and a rigid formation.
4. **D: `symbology` gives no progress.** Across a whole 1798-frame clip it wrote
   nothing to stderr for minutes, where `look --propose` writes its
   `[ n s] step…` lines. Under Slurm, a silent job can't be told from a hung one.
5. **D: the frame cache follows `TMPDIR`.** That works well: the `look` jobs
   shared `/scratch/tmp/pr135_mc/mcdonald/<clip>`, and the second one reused
   the first one's frames. But nothing documents it, and the CLAUDE.md warning
   that `/tmp` is tmpfs makes it matter here. `docs/agents.md` should say to set
   `TMPDIR` (or `--workdir`) to disk for batch jobs.
6. **Validation (works): `look --propose` found the group on its own.**
   Proposal 1 of 25 (the only "fair" one) was the cluster: 7.13 px/frame =
   213.7 px/s against the held island scene, 21° left of screen-down. The
   2026-09-18 hand workup (`pr135_transit.py`) got 212 px/s at 19°. It needed no
   hint about where or what to look for. Rows 2–25 were tower and island edges
   during the frame 312+ slew, correctly all "weak".
7. **D: the proposals sheet does not scale.** With `--more`, 25 rows came out as
   one 1172×5900 PNG. Any viewer shrinks it about 3×, so the strips can't be read.
   Tile it by ~8 rows per page, or add `--rows N`.
8. **F: `propose` merges a group into one "thing".** Proposal 1 is the cluster.
   Its members (4–6 points, 10–40 px apart) are not listed separately, so the
   `to_accept` command tracks one blended position. For formation questions
   (item 3), propose should split a proposal's members, or say that it holds
   several.
9. **B/D: `symbology` auto mode tries hue for 8 min, then exits 5 on a monochrome pointer.**
   PR135 draws north as a white "N" glyph at a fixed radius, with no colour.
   `--method auto` chose hue, ground through all 1798 frames, and then said "the
   pointer was not found". Suggestions: (a) auto should fall back to
   gradient-template matching on the brightest compact glyph at a fixed radius
   from the reticle, which is the PR135 and PR148 overlay style; (b) test the
   method on ~20 frames and fail fast before the full pass; (c) `look --frame`
   already rings the "N" (candidate 6 on frame 830), so it could print
   `--tpl-box` for it.
10. **D: `look --frame` rings the burned-in caption.** About 20 of the 61 candidates
    on frame 100 are letters of the "Public disclosure of this information is
    postponed…" caption across the bottom (frames 1–~140). The static masks miss
    it because it disappears after ~4.6 s, and `--mask-rows` would handle it.
    Caption rows are common in R06, so `look` could propose `--mask-rows` when a
    row of candidates lines up.
11. **D: the linker's "disputed frames" came from a symbology glyph.** Frames 213–225
    were flagged because the group crossed the north-pointer "N", not because the
    link was lost. The strip shows the track stayed on the group. A concern could
    say when a disputed stretch overlaps a static-mask or graphics region ("passes
    over burned-in symbology at frames …"), which would save a look.
12. **D: the `--why` text is copied into the autotrack CSV header once per mark.**
    Ten marks gave ten identical 400-character comment lines. Print it once and
    list the frames.
13. **Validation (works): `kinematics` reproduced the hand workup.** 211.8 px/s along
    202° (clockwise from screen-up), with 14/100 points sigma-clipped (the N-glyph
    crossing) and a residual of 0.5 % of the span, so UNIFORM. The hand workup
    had 212 px/s, 19° left of screen-down. The refusal to give m/s is correct and
    well worded.
14. **B-ish: `body_lengths_per_s` is quoted for an unresolved point.** It printed
    "28.6 body-lengths/s [needs no k, no R, no FOV]" from `--size-px 7.4`. But 7.4 px
    is the detector's size for a point source that is near the PSF (compare the
    single-pixel specks in the same frames), and here it is also a cluster. The
    quantity is only meaningful when the object is resolved. `kinematics` should
    compare size_px with a PSF estimate (e.g. the median size of static specks or
    stars) and put `body_lengths_per_s` in `no_power` when they match.
15. **F: the ladder has the wrong shape for a platform-motion question.** The FOV×range
    ladder gives |v_obj − v_own|⊥. For an MQ-9 flying along the line of sight, as in
    PR135 (the pointer is steady), the question is how much of the
    ground-projected rate is own-ship parallax. See item 1: a parallax ladder over
    h_O/h_A, not FOV, is what answers "is this birds?".
16. **Validation (works): `symbology --method template`.** Given `--tpl-box` around
    the "N" and `--bore`, it solved 600/600 frames with r = 198.44 ± 0.54 px
    (0.27 %), in 4 min on 1 CPU. The per-window rotation and parallax-sense text
    is exactly what's needed.
17. **D: template positions look integer-quantized.** In the 0–10.8 s window,
    r_sd = 8.5e-14 and resid = 3.5e-15 deg: every frame returned the identical
    integer position. That is honest for a static glyph, but it means the angle
    resolution is 1 px / r = 0.29°, which is never stated. A sub-pixel peak fit
    (parabolic on the NCC surface) would help, and `results` should carry
    `theta_resolution_deg` so a "no measurable rotation" can be quoted with its
    bound.
18. **D: the absolute theta depends on `--bore`, and nothing warns about it.** The 2026-09-18
    hand tool (a different boresight estimate) read 40.38°, `mcdonald` with the
    frame-830 reticle reads 41.73°. A 1.3° offset from a ~4 px boresight
    difference is expected at r ≈ 198, but the output should say that θ is only
    as good as the boresight (±atan(σ_bore/r)).
19. **B (serious): `layers` registers the sensor's fixed-pattern column banding, not the scene.**
    On PR135 frames 150–320 (the island is held, and drifts ~10 px/s on screen),
    `layers --auto-track` reported one "isotropic" layer moving 0.01–0.05 px per
    5-frame baseline, i.e. screen-fixed. The island's hot building (which the
    auto-track locked onto, item 20) then read **10 px/s "against the background"**
    although it *is* the background. Its CSV track goes x 442 → 388 over 5.6 s,
    which is the real scene drift the 2026-09-18 gradient-NCC tool measured
    (−9.0, −4.2 px/s). The 5-frame ZNCC templates on a low-texture IR scene lock
    onto the vertical stripes, which are the strongest texture and are
    stationary on the sensor. Consequences: any `layers`/`propose` "against the
    background" rate on a banded IR clip is really "against the screen", and a
    tracking segment reads as "camera still" whatever the gimbal does. Fix ideas:
    (a) destripe before registration: subtract each frame's column median (and
    row median), or high-pass along x only; (b) a self-test: if the fitted layer
    shift is ≈ 0 while the frame-to-frame difference image has structure that
    moves, warn; (c) take the static-pattern estimate `forensics.static_pattern`
    already computes and subtract it. This would have silently corrupted the
    tracking-segment "is the direction change camera motion?" test, so I did
    not run it.
20. **D: `--auto-track` took the island's hot building.** That is the documented
    behaviour (it takes the strongest source), and the strip showed it at once,
    so the gate worked. But when `mark` has already saved `<tag>_marks.json` in
    the case directory, `layers` could use it by default, or at least say "a
    marks file exists here; pass `--marks`", instead of starting blind.
21. **F: photometry and flicker (wingbeat).** "Is it birds?" is a standing
    question (PR135 here; the council asks it of most multi-point clips). The
    package has no per-object light curve or periodogram. I wrote
    `cases/pr135/group_photometry.py` on top of `forensics.source_candidates`,
    `frame_mask` and `Clip`. It needs: a 3×3 core minus annulus-median light curve
    per linked track; a Hann periodogram with the resolution printed;
    **mandatory controls**, namely screen-fixed specks (sensor defects) and
    background apertures riding with the object; and **the codec's cadence marked
    and tested**. Here the GOP is P-B-B-B, so 30/4 = 7.49 Hz, and a
    constant-brightness moving dot re-encoded the same way is the control
    (`codec_flicker_control.py`). The periodicity found in PR135 sits at that
    cadence (item 23). Without the codec control, anyone would announce a
    7.5 Hz "wingbeat".
22. **F: flag screen-fixed hot pixels as sensor defects.** 10 of the 18 ≥90-frame
    tracks in the tracking segment were sensor defects: pairwise separations constant to ≤1 px over
    100–300 frames while the camera slews. `propose` rightly ranks them low, but a
    per-clip "defect map" (sources whose positions are fixed on the sensor across
    frames where the scene moves) would help `look`, `propose`, the linker and
    the item-19 destriping all at once.
23. **F: encoding metadata in the envelope.** `clip` has fps_exact but not the GOP
    structure (I/P/B pattern and period, has_b_frames, bitrate). For flicker and for
    content-cadence (hold-and-jump) checks, the P-frame period is a first-class
    systematic. `ffprobe -show_frames -show_entries frame=pict_type` is cheap;
    put `gop` in `clip`.
24. **Trap for the flicker feature (item 21), found by the control: pixel phase.** A 3×3
    core re-centred on the *rounded* centroid modulates a sub-pixel-moving
    PSF of FWHM ~3 px by several percent, at fps × frac(v). The planted dots flickered
    at 3–6 Hz *before* encoding. The fix, which the package should hard-wire: a
    radius ≥ 4 px aperture with sub-pixel (supersampled) weights, centred on a
    5-frame-smoothed track (`phot_bigap.py`). With it, the planted dots show nothing
    at 7.49 Hz before or after an H.264 re-encode matching PR135's GOP
    (≤ 4.5× median), while the real members show 13–25 % at 7.0–7.9 Hz. **Also
    required: a common-window cross-spectrum.** Members at different frequencies
    and phases over the *same* frames (#1456 at 7.00 Hz against #1411/#1491/#1492 at
    7.74–7.82 Hz, phases 84–175° apart) are what rule out a cadence of the video.
    Make that the test's pass condition.
25. *(withdrawn: the OOM kill was my own script caching float64 frames; `clip._load`
    already returns float32.)*
