# Audit before the next release: the window, and where the time goes (2026-09-26)

Jacob, while Ravi tries 0.2.9: "please do an audit of the GUI and computational
efficiency for the next release. This should look and feel like modern software."

How it was done. Every screen of the window was opened on PR113 380–440 (the
Technical Note's transit, 61 frames) offscreen, photographed and timed, in a
4-CPU Slurm job standing in for a laptop (`/scratch/mcdonald/audit-2026-09-26/shots/`,
one PNG a screen; the script is `audit_gui.py` beside them). The same clip went
through `mcdonald run` with the curated track, every stage timed. Then the
functions the time goes to were profiled one by one on real frames, and wherever
a faster way existed it was written as a prototype and **checked against the
shipped function for identical output** before it is called a gain here. Nothing
in the package was changed; this document is the deliverable, and section 1 is
the order I would do the work in.

**§5.2 -- the ZNCC's window variance, in float64, from the whole frame's summed-area
tables.** What was wrong was found exactly, and was worse than §4.5 supposed. `zncc`
normalised each placement by `s2 - s1²/N` with both sums from a float32 cumulative sum
over the 618-px search window; on PR113's band-passed sky that cumulative sum reaches
2.6 × 10⁷, where one float32 step is 2, so a flat sky patch's own sum of squares (1.7,
1.3 at two placements checked against a two-pass float64 reference) came out as 0.001
(the floor) and 2.3. Divided by a floored norm, a template of sky then "correlated" at
13.66 and 9.47 -- values a correlation cannot take -- at shifts anywhere in the reach:
the pair 400/405 had 98 "good" rows of 165 whose shifts spread 130 px either way and
agreed on nothing. `shift_field` now takes three summed-area tables of frame b in
float64 (`integral`, `box_of`: the band-passed frame, its square, the mask) once a pair,
and hands `zncc` each placement's norm; the sums agree with the reference to four
decimals. On that pair: 148 rows, 16 good, peaks 0.999 where 13.66 was; with zero
allowed, the consensus at zero from 25 templates where 9 were. On PR144 300-500's
textured sea and cloud (pair 350/355): 153 of 160 shifts identical to 0.05 px, the
consensus (-64.09, -12.88) from 52 templates identical; the 7 that moved were
low-variance patches. `layers` on PR113 380-440: the same held-still shares (38 %, and

### 3.2 Frames, the player and the cache (fine)

A 1080p PNG frame (0.36 MB) decodes in 61 ms with PIL (the stages) and 41 ms
with Qt (the window). Raw frames would read in 1.3 ms but cost 6 MB each, 17×
the disk, for a decode that is 2–10 % of any stage: **keep PNG**. The window's
cache (4–6 decode threads, 768 MB) plays PR113 at full rate on 4 CPUs (60
frames shown, none skipped) and steps a frame in 6–7 ms. The segment player's
reader alone delivers 30 fps on 4 CPUs (119 frames in 4 s; ffmpeg's own
ceiling is ~80 fps here).

### 3.3 Find

`propose._frame` is 2.3 s a frame in one process: gaussian filters 0.74 s (the
scale ladder, on crops), five frames decoded to grey 0.65 s, the still-again
check 0.40 s, the peak search 0.24 s. On 4 CPUs, 57 frames took **102 s** in
the window (masks ~25 s, a pool start for each block of 90 frames, the search,
`points_in` per proposal per block, the strips on the GUI thread). After 1, 2
and 4: about 45 s.

### 3.4 Follow

74 s on 4 CPUs for a track of four frames, because the sweep tries spot sizes
5…45 on two frames in both polarities and then runs the chosen size (21 px, 4.3
s a frame) on all 61 frames. The detector's cost is the kernel's: direct
correlation with a 2r+1 square, r = 1.35 × size + 1 (§4.3). By FFT every size
costs ~0.4 s: Follow ≈ 15 s.

| **whole run** | **640 s (10:40) with integrity, ~440 s without** | for a 2-second segment, on 4 CPUs |

Measure from the window, integrity off, on the same clip: 84 s — but with the layers templates read from the cache the command-line run had just written beside the frames (`bg_layers_*.npz`); a first Measure pays the command line's 315 s there. To the track-sheet question: 72 s, all of it masks, survey, symbology and the sheet.

### 3.6 Pools

`progress.pooled` makes a `multiprocessing.Pool` per stage with the default
start method. On Python ≤ 3.13 Linux that was fork (cheap, and unsafe from a
window with threads — the linker's `_Workers` avoids it on purpose). On **Python
3.14 the default is forkserver** (this machine), on macOS/Windows spawn: each
worker imports numpy, scipy and mcdonald afresh. Measured from an entry point
like `mcdonald run`, 4 workers: **2.9 s a pool, every pool** (spawn: 2.8 s). With
`multiprocessing.set_forkserver_preload([the worker modules])` before the first
pool: 1.9 s once, then 0.05 s a pool. The linker's own forkserver context shares
the server, so Follow gains too. Windows has no forkserver: there a pool kept
alive for the session would be the equivalent (not written; Gary's test first).
One more: `propose.search` starts a pool per block of 90 frames.

A side effect met in this audit: any script that calls the package's pools
needs the `if __name__ == "__main__":` guard under 3.14, or forkserver
re-imports it and fails ("bootstrapping phase"). The package's entry points and
tests have it.

### 3.7 Memory

`static_masks` peaks at 2.6 GB (three 40-frame stacks and the temporaries of
`std(0)` over one of them); the window makes it on its own thread, so the window
process alone can reach 3.5 GB while Find starts. Reduced to 1.6 GB by
reductions in column blocks (bit-identical, checked) and chroma kept as uint8.
The pools' workers hold a few float32 frames each (8 MB a frame as grey, 25 as
RGB); 4–8 workers are fine on 8 GB.

## 4. The fixes, each with its measurement and its check

### 4.1 Grey, chroma, and the frame mask (exact)

`rgb` is float32 H×W×3. `rgb.mean(2)` reduces over an axis of length 3, which
numpy does slowly (61 ms); `(r + g + b) / 3` is the same float32 arithmetic in
the same order: **bit-identical, 11 ms**. `rgb.max(2) - rgb.min(2)` 235 ms →
`np.maximum(np.maximum(r, g), b) - np.minimum(...)` 16 ms, identical.
`frame_mask` (called on every frame of every stage and link) is 287 ms of which
235 is that chroma: → ~70 ms. `static_masks` 23.4 → 10.9 s with every output
array identical. Places: `clip.grey`, `forensics.frame_mask`, `static_masks`,
`symbology.py:68,92`, and the `.mean(2)` in layers, integrity, flicker, groups,
tracksheet (grep `.mean(2)`).

### 4.2 Pools (exact)

In `progress.py`, before the first pool: on POSIX use the forkserver context
with `set_forkserver_preload(["mcdonald.forensics", "mcdonald.layers",
"mcdonald.propose", "mcdonald.integrity", "mcdonald.tracksheet",
"mcdonald.groups", "mcdonald.flicker", "mcdonald.symbology"])`; on Windows
spawn as now. `autolink._Workers` already uses forkserver and then shares the
preloaded server. `propose.search`: one pool for the search, `imap` over all
frames, yielding per block as now. Measured: 2.9 s → 0.05 s a pool.

### 4.3 The detector by FFT (identical spots)

`source_candidates` correlates the frame with a disc-minus-annulus kernel,
`ndimage.correlate(img, kernel, mode="nearest")`: 29×29 taps at size 9, 125×125
at 45 (32 GMAC a frame). Pad the frame by the kernel's radius with `mode="edge"`
and `fftconvolve(padded, kernel[::-1, ::-1], mode="valid")`: the same response
to 2 × 10⁻⁷ relative, and on PR113 frame 400 **the same spots, in the same
order, at the same sub-pixel positions** at every size. Keep the direct
correlation at size 5 (276 ms direct, 406 by FFT); FFT from 9 up. Every size
then costs 0.34–0.47 s. Hold it against `tools/find_rank.py --link` (rank and
link, the nine cases) and `tests/test_golden.py`: the 2 × 10⁻⁷ is below any
threshold in the linker (`MIN_RESP`, `LIKE`, `STRONGER` 5 %) but the rule is to
look.

### 4.4 Bandpass once (exact)

`shift_field_auto` → `still_score` filters both frames (0.8 s), then
`shift_field` filters them again (0.7 s). Filter in `shift_field_auto` and pass
`ha, hb` down: a layers pair 6.4 → 5.3 s; an integrity `_pair1` 1.0 → 0.65 s
(and its consecutive pairs share a frame each: a per-worker cache of the last
filtered frame would take another 0.3 s off each).

### 4.5 What the registration cannot lose (and one correctness note)

The rest of `shift_field` is 183 FFT correlations of a 128-px template over a
618-px window (2.3 s) — the algorithm, not waste; coarse-to-fine would be 5×
faster but changes which peak is found and cannot measure the runner-up `pk2`
over the whole window, which `good()` needs. Left alone. The 556 per-window box
sums (2.0 s) can come from three integral images of the whole frame (0.5 s
saved), **but** that computes the ZNCC's variance in float64 where the shipped
code sums squares in float32 over a 618² window and subtracts — which on
featureless sky (PR113) cancels catastrophically: the prototype kept 148 of the
165 template rows, the others being flat-sky templates whose shipped
normalisation is rounding noise. `good()` (peak ≥ 0.5, gap ≥ 0.05) drops most
such rows either way, but the fix is not bit-identical and needs golden and the
recorded-track table before it ships. A correctness improvement first, a speed
one second; Jacob's call (§5).

### 4.6 The window

- `QtMarker.__init__`: `resize` to `min(1500, 0.9·w) × min(920, 0.9·h)` of
  `screen().availableGeometry()`; `restoreGeometry`/`restoreState` from
  QSettings on open, save on close; the same for the report page (960×920),
  Getting started (720×820), the overview (1180×720).
- `show_work`: sizes `[0.62, 0.38]`; FindPanel: hide the list's scroll area
  until the first result; a "Looking…" line in its place.
- `Loupe`: draw a first state in `__init__` (a dark square with the words), or
  show the frame's centre when the window opens.
- `FindPanel._show`: strips from the search thread (`propose.strip` in `job()`,
  passed with the proposals), the GUI thread only wraps pixmaps.
- `dock.setTitleBarWidget(QtWidgets.QWidget())`.
- `choose_start`: a "Recent" list (up to five, from QSettings `parts/*`), and
  `setAcceptDrops(True)` with a `dropEvent` on the first screen and the window.
- `gui.main`: show the first screen at once; `offer_update` from a 250-ms QTimer
  polling `check.found`, so the network never delays the first paint.
- Retina: the media icons are Qt's raster 16/32-px standard pixmaps, and the

### 4.7 The test rig (developer-facing)

`tests/test_gui.py`'s `settle` and `wait_for` use `QTest.qWait`, which holds the
GIL for its whole wait: a background thread ran at 0.04 M loops/s under it
against 7.1 M under `app.exec()` or a `processEvents()` + `sleep(0.002)` loop.
So every wait in the suite stalls the very threads it waits for, the suite runs
long (331 s here, 939 s on the Mac) and its timing checks flake. Replace the two
waits with the processEvents loop; expect a faster and steadier suite, and
retire `MCDONALD_TEST_PATIENCE` if it holds.
